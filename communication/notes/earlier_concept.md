# Earlier article concept and decisions

In June 2026 the agreed question was: **“Can neutrino beams and modern detector technologies enable practical direct communication through the Earth, and under what technological and physical constraints?”** The initial working title was *Towards Global Through-Earth Communication Using Neutrino Beams*. The intention was a publishable quantitative feasibility study, with accelerator-generated beams, large detectors, link budgets, data rates, and energy efficiency.

The initial outline covered introduction, neutrino physics, system architecture, link budget, case studies, comparison with conventional communication, future technologies and conclusion. Example use cases discussed were a Stockholm–Sydney path, Arctic links, submerged receivers and Earth–Mars occultation. The prior discussion also mentioned disaster recovery and extreme environments. These are **research questions**, not results. The 2026-09 first draft narrows its actual calculation to fixed terrestrial chords; none of the named locations, mobile receivers or planetary routes has yet been modelled.

The initial rough interaction estimate was approximately 10⁻³⁸ cm² at GeV energies and `N_events = flux × cross section × targets × efficiency`. The new model supplies explicit geometry and a selected-event error target, while retaining important untested assumptions. Before extending the case studies, select source hardware, physical beam divergence, neutrino spectrum, detector response and a complete message protocol.

**Bibliographic correction:** June notes incorrectly attributed Stancil et al. (2012) to *Physical Review Letters* 109, 070802. Its actual journal reference is *Modern Physics Letters A* 27, 1250077, DOI 10.1142/S0217732312500770. “Learned (1994)” and “Winter (2020)” appeared in early notes without verified titles or precise relevance; they are not treated as established references here. Verified works appear in `../../background/bibliography.md`.
