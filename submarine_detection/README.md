# Earlier article: submarine antineutrino detection

**Sven-Patrik Hallsjö (2026), [*Locating nuclear-powered submarines with antineutrinos*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15642), arXiv:2605.15642v1.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15642v1) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15642)

This is the canonical public version of the earlier article. Its original TeX, figures and data files were not available in this workspace, so they have not been reconstructed or represented as originals.

The paper asks whether reactor antineutrinos from a moving submarine can be detected by a barrier of large detectors. It models signal versus passing distance, detector mass, depth, and background; evaluates a Poisson detection operating point; and compares narrow passages with broad approaches. Its abstract reports a 20 kt Gibraltar benchmark `Z_A ≈ 2.54` for an assumed 100 MW thermal source, and about `Z_A ≈ 4.66` for a three-detector line under its stated assumptions. Those values are **from the published article** and are not outputs of this repository's communication model.

The communication draft builds on the practice of explicit geometry and sparse-event statistical accounting, while studying a controlled, modulated GeV neutrino source and a bit-decoding problem. Its source, interaction and detector assumptions are materially different from the MeV antineutrino inverse-beta-decay case in this earlier paper.
