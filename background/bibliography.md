# Working bibliography (verified links, 23 September 2026)

This is the collection identified for the two article themes to date, not an exhaustive systematic review. Exact bibliographic details should be checked before submission.

## Author's previous work

1. S.-P. Hallsjö, “Locating nuclear-powered submarines with antineutrinos” (2026), [arXiv:2605.15642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15642). Sparse-event detection, geometry, detector deployment and Poisson operating points.
2. S.-P. Hallsjö, *Charged current quasi-elastic muon neutrino interactions in the Baby MIND detector*, University of Glasgow PhD thesis (2018), [institutional record](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/41123/). Reconstruction, efficiencies, accelerator neutrino interactions and nuSTORM context.

## Direct communication and adjacent applications

3. D. D. Stancil et al., “Demonstration of Communication using Neutrinos,” *Modern Physics Letters A* **27**, 1250077 (2012), [doi:10.1142/S0217732312500770](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0217732312500770), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.2847). Measured NuMI–MINERvA link.
4. P. Huber, “Submarine neutrino communication,” *Physics Letters B* **692**, 268–271 (2010), [doi:10.1016/j.physletb.2010.08.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physletb.2010.08.003), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4554). Proposed one-way muon-storage-ring submarine link.
5. J. G. Learned, S. Pakvasa and A. Zee, “[Galactic Neutrino Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2429)” (2008). Very long-distance communication concept.
6. J. Fidalgo Prieto et al., “[Submarine Navigation using Neutrinos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09231)” (2022). Navigation and timing, distinct from payload delivery.

## Source, propagation, interactions and receiver references

7. DUNE Collaboration, “[Far Detector Technical Design Report, Volume I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02967)” (2020). Stationary large-detector reference.
8. Hyper-Kamiokande Proto-Collaboration, “[Hyper-Kamiokande Design Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04163)” (2018). Water-Cherenkov technology.
9. “[Neutrinos from Stored Muons (nuSTORM)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06137)” (2025). Controlled-beam source design.
10. Particle Data Group, “[Neutrino Cross Section Measurements](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-nu-cross-sections.pdf)” (2025). Interaction inputs for spectrum integration.
11. “[Neutrino Oscillations through the Earth's Core](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01148)” (2021). Earth matter effects relevant to long chords.

## Reading and validation priorities

- Review references and citing works for items 3–6; identify any recent through-Earth link budgets that answer the same question.
- Extract actual beam energy–angle distributions, source-to-neutrino efficiency and duty cycle for a chosen design.
- Replace the illustrative cross section and unity survival with energy-dependent sources and Earth propagation.
- Compare experimental **decoded** throughput with the simulated **raw** error model on a matched protocol.

## Deep-space low-rate protocol design (analogy, not neutrino performance evidence)

12. J. I. Statman, “[Optimizing the Galileo Space Communication Link](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-116/116k.html),” *Interplanetary Network Progress Report* 42-116, 114–120 (1994). Compression, antenna arraying, coding and reprocessing on a weak radio link.
13. B. Moision and J. Hamkins, “[Deep-Space Optical Communications Downlink Budget: Modulation and Coding](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-154/154K.html),” *Interplanetary Network Progress Report* 42-154, 1–28 (2003). Photon-counting capacity, PPM and error-control codes under power constraints.
14. J. I. Statman et al., “[Decoder synchronization for deep space missions](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19940025161)” (1994). Frame and node acquisition at low rate and low SNR.
